Engine Documentation

How Chessplate's engine actually works.

Search Pipeline

  • Root move generation starts from all legal moves.
  • Iterative deepening runs from depth 1 up to an Elo-dependent depth cap.
  • Each depth uses negamax with alpha-beta pruning to skip losing branches fast.
  • If a position is tactical or noisy, quiescence search extends only captures/check sequences.

Move Ordering

  • Promotions are ranked highest.
  • Captures are sorted with a simple MVV/LVA-style heuristic.
  • Castling gets a bonus to improve king safety development.
  • Best moves from previous iteration are searched first at the next depth.

Evaluation Model

  • Material values: pawn 100, knight 320, bishop 330, rook 500, queen 900.
  • Piece-square tables reward active placement and center control.
  • Bishop pair bonus is applied for both colors.
  • A small side-to-move initiative bonus is added to reduce passivity.

Elo Simulation

  • The Elo slider maps to depth, time budget, and node budget.
  • Lower Elo injects bounded centipawn noise into top candidates.
  • Higher Elo reduces noise and increases search resources.
  • Range is configured from 600 to 2800 in the app UI.

Live Telemetry: Search Prism

Search Prism displays depth growth bars, evaluation trend, node throughput, stability, volatility, and candidate move lead gap while the engine searches.

This lets you see confidence and instability in real time instead of only seeing a final move choice.

How To Play Chess

Quick rules and fundamentals for new players.

Goal

Win by checkmating the opponent king: attack it so it has no legal escape, block, or capture response.

Piece Movement

  • King: one square any direction.
  • Queen: any number of squares, straight or diagonal.
  • Rook: any number of squares, straight lines.
  • Bishop: any number of squares, diagonals.
  • Knight: L-shape jumps (2 + 1), can hop over pieces.
  • Pawn: one step forward, captures diagonally; two-step from starting rank.

Special Rules

  • Castling: king + rook move together once if squares are clear and not under attack.
  • En passant: a pawn can capture a two-step pawn as if it moved one square, immediately on next move only.
  • Promotion: a pawn reaching the last rank becomes queen, rook, bishop, or knight.

Game Results

  • Checkmate: win for the attacking side.
  • Stalemate: draw when side to move has no legal moves but is not in check.
  • Fifty-move rule: draw if no pawn move or capture happens for fifty moves each side.

Starter Principles

  • Develop knights and bishops early; do not move the same piece repeatedly without reason.
  • Castle early to protect your king and connect rooks.
  • Fight for center squares and avoid hanging pieces to one-move tactics.
  • Before every move, check: opponent threats, your checks/captures, king safety.
